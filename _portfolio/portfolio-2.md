In the 17th century, the springs at Nine Wells, Cambridge fed a conduit built by Thomas Hobbes (of horse choice fame) to provide clean drinking water to the city. This report explores the origin and evolution of the aqueous geochemistry at Nine Wells, arising from an interplay between the carbonate lithology of Cambridge and the interaction of the spring water with the atmosphere as it emerges from the ground.
